Let it "cook"

Let the wetted mix "cook" for 1-2 weeks before transplanting. Microbes (Dr. Earth includes beneficial microbes and mycorrhizae) break amendments into plant-available nutrients, and early nitrogen, heat, pH and moisture settle so seedling roots aren't burned. Keep it moist (not soggy), covered and warm; a little earthy smell is normal.

More bags?

Multiply every amount by the number of bags.

2 Tbspper 5-gallon mix

Good soakthen no more

How to feed

Shake the bottle well. Mix 2 Tbsp (1 fl oz) of concentrate into a 5-gallon bucket of water, then give the bag a good soaking from that mix. Mix only what you'll use; don't store the diluted mix. A fishy smell is normal. If you use another Neptune's product, follow that label.

When

Give one feed, 4 weeks after planting, then no more Neptune's. If week 4 lands in the top-dress week, push that feed back one week. This is the only Neptune's feed in the grow.

Why stay conservative

This 2 Tbsp per 5-gallon mix is well under the label rate, so it's gentle. The soil is already amended with Dr. Earth plus compost, and a top-dress comes later. Autoflowers are light feeders, so one soak is enough.

  2. Mix into ~13 cups of base soil.
  3. Scratch the top inch of the bag.
  4. Spread 1.5-2 in, off the stem.
  5. Water in gently.

That week

Ease off Dr. Earth Bloom. If the week-4 Neptune's feed lands this week, push it back one week.

Round two

3-4 weeks later, only if it lands before the final 2-3 weeks.

Within label rates

Guano: 5 Tbsp in 5 gal = 1 Tbsp/gal, the label rate. Langbeinite: 6 tsp = ~1.2 tsp/gal, inside 1-2 tsp/gal.

Watch For

Burnt or crispy tips

Too strong. Back off feeding, water plain.

Pale or yellow leaves early

Check watering and pH before adding more.

Lower leaves yellow late in flower

Normal fade. Leave it.
